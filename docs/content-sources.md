# Content sources policy

All nutrition, allergen, feeding-age and food-safety content in Chicken Little comes **only from government sources**. Nothing safety-critical is written from memory or generated by AI; the AI assistant may only retrieve and combine approved entries (PRD §15). Every safety card in the app shows its source.

## Approved domains

| Domain | Agency | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| canada.ca | Health Canada | Priority allergen list, infant feeding (6–24 months), textures, iron-rich first foods |
| cdc.gov | US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention | When/how to start solids, allergen introduction timing, choking hazards |
| fda.gov | US Food and Drug Administration | US major food allergens |
| nih.gov / niaid.nih.gov | US National Institutes of Health | Peanut allergy prevention guidance |
| usda.gov (incl. fdc.nal.usda.gov) | US Department of Agriculture | Nutrient data (FoodData Central) |

Other sites (non-profits, hospitals, blogs, commercial apps) are **not** approved sources, even when reputable.

## Sources used so far

- Health Canada — Food allergies (11 priority allergens: eggs, milk, mustard, peanuts, crustaceans and molluscs, fish, sesame seeds, soy, sulphites, tree nuts, wheat and triticale).
  https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-nutrition/food-safety/food-allergies-intolerances/food-allergies.html
- Health Canada — Nutrition for Healthy Term Infants, 6 to 24 months (iron-rich first foods; lumpy textures no later than 9 months; avoid hard, small-and-round, smooth-and-sticky foods; supervise eating).
  https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/canada-food-guide/resources/infant-feeding/nutrition-healthy-term-infants-recommendations-birth-six-months/6-24-months.html
- CDC — When, What, and How to Introduce Solid Foods (start at about 6 months, not before 4; introduce potentially allergenic foods when introducing other foods; one single-ingredient food at a time, 3–5 days apart).
  https://www.cdc.gov/infant-toddler-nutrition/foods-and-drinks/when-what-and-how-to-introduce-solid-foods.html
- CDC — Choking Hazards (incl. spoonfuls/chunks of nut and seed butters, whole nuts, uncut grapes; prepare food to the right shape, size and texture).
  https://www.cdc.gov/infant-toddler-nutrition/foods-and-drinks/choking-hazards.html
- FDA — Food Allergies (9 major US allergens incl. sesame since Jan 1, 2023).
  https://www.fda.gov/food/food-labeling-nutrition/food-allergies

## Decisions made from these sources

- **Allergen dashboard list (Canada):** Health Canada's priority allergens. Sulphites are a labelled additive, not a food that's "introduced", so they're excluded from introduction tracking and noted on screen. A US region setting would use the FDA's 9.
- **Peanut butter in recipes:** always "thinned and stirred in", never served by the spoonful (CDC choking hazards).
- **Disclaimer:** introduction tracking is not a medical guarantee; reactions route to a healthcare provider (PRD §8).

# Content sources policy

All nutrition, allergen, feeding-age and food-safety content in Chicken Little comes **only from government sources**. Nothing safety-critical is written from memory or generated by AI. Every safety statement in the app shows its source, linked.

## Approved domains

| Domain | Agency |
|---|---|
| canada.ca | Health Canada |
| healthlinkbc.ca | HealthLink BC (Government of British Columbia) |
| cdc.gov | US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention |
| fda.gov | US Food and Drug Administration |
| usda.gov (fns, fna, wicworks, fdc.nal) | US Department of Agriculture |
| nih.gov / niaid.nih.gov | US National Institutes of Health |

Other sites (non-profits, hospitals, professional societies, blogs, commercial apps) are **not** approved, even when reputable.

## Where the content lives

- `tools/research/foods.json`: the raw research: 25 government pages, 37 general guidance statements, 10 allergens, 36 foods. Each statement lists the source ids it came from.
- `tools/build_data.py`: turns the research into `js/data.js`, applies the corrections from an independent fact-check (below), and fails if any statement has no valid source.
- In the app, **Baby → Sources** lists every source page.

## Fact-check

A second, independent pass loaded every source URL and checked the safety-critical claims against the pages. Corrections applied by the build script:

- Vitamin D: supplement applies to babies who get any breast milk; only exclusively formula-fed babies don't need one.
- Fish: Health Canada's 75 g/month limit is stated for 12 to 24 months, not for babies.
- Peanut thinning ratio (1 tbsp + 1 tbsp): HealthLink BC only. NIH's recipe differs, so it isn't cited for this.
- Choking first aid: wording aligned with the parent-facing pages.
- Removed citations that didn't support their sentence; dropped one HealthLink BC link that resolved to a search page.
- Shrimp: no shellfish-specific government preparation guidance exists; the app says so and uses the general meat guidance.

## Decisions made from these sources

- **Allergen list:** Health Canada's priority allergens. Sulphites are a labelled additive, not a food that's introduced, so they're not tracked (stated on screen).
- **Canada vs US differences** (cow's milk timing, waiting days between new foods, juice, plant drinks, mercury lists) are shown side by side, labelled.
- **"Established"** is set by the parent, never inferred. The app says it isn't a medical guarantee and points to a health care provider for reactions or higher-risk babies.
- **Reminders** use HealthLink BC's advice to keep tolerated allergens in the diet regularly (several times a week): the nudge appears after 7 days without a serving.
- **Ideas** only combine foods; every step shown is that food's own sourced preparation text for the baby's age.
- **Nutrition** is fetched live from USDA FoodData Central and shown exactly as published (per 100 g), with a link to the USDA record.
